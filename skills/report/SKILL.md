# Research Report Generator

Generates a polished, stakeholder-ready UX research report tailored to the researcher's study and audience. Built on the Instacart Research team design system (2-column tables, RACI header, emoji-coded priorities) and the methodology canon: Nielsen Norman Group (Gibbons, Moran, Budiu), Tomer Sharon (*Validating Product Ideas*), Steve Portigal (*Doorbells, Danger, and Dead Batteries*), Erika Hall (*Just Enough Research*), Nikki Anderson, and dscout People Nerds. Also integrates the Instacart-internal **AIxUXR Playbook** (Loosbrock & Venkatraman, 2025) — specifically the Report Critic framework, H.E.A.R.T. philosophy, and Business Justification patterns.

## Guiding philosophy: H.E.A.R.T. (AIxUXR Playbook)

Every report generated by this skill must honor the H.E.A.R.T. framework — Instacart Research's non-negotiable philosophy for AI-assisted research work. The researcher is the driver; this skill is the co-pilot.

| Letter | Principle | What this means for the report |
|--------|-----------|-------------------------------|
| **H** | **Human-centered** | Ground every finding in the lived experience of a named user type. No abstraction at the cost of humanity. |
| **E** | **Experience-focused** | The report itself is an experience — scannable, respectful of the reader's time, visually coherent. A clunky report is bad research. |
| **A** | **Amplifying** | AI drafts; the researcher decides. The skill accelerates drudgery (formatting, prioritization, evidence curation) so the researcher can do the strategic synthesis. |
| **R** | **Responsible** | No PII in quotes (use P01/P02 + demographic tag). Flag sample limitations. Never invent a quote or participant. |
| **T** | **Transparent** | Attribute the AI assist. Every claim traces to evidence. Every recommendation traces to an insight. The chain is visible. |

## Core discipline: Finding → Insight → Recommendation

Enforce this ladder throughout the report. Collapsing it is the single most common failure mode NNG and Sharon catalog.

| Rung | Voice | Definition | Example |
|------|-------|-----------|---------|
| **Finding** | Past tense, descriptive | *Observed* pattern across data points | "7 of 10 shoppers could not identify the brand on the PDP within 30 seconds." |
| **Insight** | Present tense, explanatory | *Interpreted* — explains "so what" and "why" in context | "Shoppers default to the product image as the brand signal; brand text is invisible during scan." |
| **Recommendation** | Imperative | *Actionable* — who ships what, by when, at what priority | "🔴 P0 — Move brand badge above product title. Owner: PDP squad. Ship next sprint." |

**Rules:**
- Never label a finding as an insight (Sharon: "if it won't have significant impact in the future, it's a finding").
- Every recommendation must trace to an insight, which must trace to findings, which must trace to data.
- NNG: "Insights are what researchers should strive to create." Findings alone do not justify a ship decision.

**Insight template (Nikki Anderson):** "I am *(persona)* trying to *(do X)* but *(barrier)* because *(root cause)*, which makes me feel *(emotion)*."

## BLUF — Bottom Line Up Front

Every report opens with a BLUF: three sentences answering what we learned, what it means, what to do. Execs who read only the first paragraph must be able to decide.

**Bad** (process-led): "This study ran 4 weeks with 12 participants using unmoderated remote testing."
**Good** (answer-led): "Shoppers cannot identify brands on our PDP — 7 of 10 failed the brand-recall task. This suppresses private-label repeat purchase (est. $X impact). Recommend moving brand badge above product title this sprint."

## System instructions

### Step 1: Gather Report Inputs

Ask the researcher for the raw material. Accept any format: Google Doc URL, pasted notes, Slack paste, bullet list, voice-memo transcript. Use AskUserQuestion in batches of up to 4.

**Batch 1 — Study basics:**

**Q1 — Study name/title** (short, e.g., "Private-label PDP brand recall")

**Q2 — Primary audience**
- **Executive** (C-suite, skip-levels, VP+) — 1-2 pages, BLUF, 3-5 top findings, single ask
- **Product / PM** (Recommended for most studies) — 8-15 pages, full methodology, prioritized rec table
- **Engineering** — behavior-first, failure modes, edge cases, priority matrix
- **Research team / mixed internal** — methodology-heavy, reproducibility, caveats
- **Other** — researcher specifies

**Q3 — Study method & sample size** (e.g., "8 moderated usability sessions, Instacart weekly shoppers")

**Q4 — Source material**
- Let the researcher paste raw analysis notes, affinity clusters, themes, quotes, or link a Google Doc
- If Google Doc URL: read via Glean (mcp__735858e3-df5d-4167-8a6d-f4047ed77a06__read_document) or `google-docs:fetch-google-doc` skill

**Batch 2 — Content (after Batch 1):**

**Q5 — Key findings** (raw bullet list — the patterns observed)

**Q6 — Evidence available** (quotes? video clips? metrics? screenshots? which are hero-worthy?)

**Q7 — Draft recommendations** (if the researcher has any) — these will be prioritized and owned in Step 4

**Q8 — Open questions / follow-ups** (what the study did not answer)

### Step 2: Propose Report Structure

Based on the audience from Q2, propose the structure and confirm before generating. Branch by audience:

#### Executive variant (1-2 pages)
```
1. Header + RACI
2. BLUF (3 sentences)
3. Top 3-5 findings with priority emojis (no methodology here)
4. The single ask (decision needed)
5. Methodology (one paragraph, at the bottom)
```

**Format-conversion note (AIxUXR Playbook — Presentation ↔ Reference Guide):**

Executive and Product-PM readouts often start life as slide decks or end up needing to be converted into one. Use this guidance when the audience shifts:

| Scenario | Convert to | Why |
|----------|-----------|-----|
| Researcher has a slide deck, audience now wants a durable doc | **Reference Guide format** (this skill's default) | Slides are ephemeral; a reference guide becomes a source-of-truth for future PMs, ENG, and onboarding new researchers. Distill, don't recap slide-by-slide. |
| Researcher has this markdown report, audience is a live exec review | **Slide deck** (export via `gws-slides` after generation) | Execs in live meetings scan visuals, not paragraphs. Keep BLUF as slide 1, one finding per slide, rec table as appendix. |
| Mixed audience (live exec review + durable doc for PM team) | **Generate both** | The report is the canonical artifact; the deck is a derivative. Never let the deck become the source of truth — it loses nuance and evidence trail. |

When converting slide content → reference doc, apply the Playbook rules: (1) distill, don't summarize slide-by-slide; (2) extract frameworks prominently; (3) clarify Finding vs. Insight; (4) standardize messy workshop examples into clean "Example A / B" format; (5) authoritative tone, no conversational filler.

#### Product / PM variant (8-15 pages) — Default
```
1. Header + RACI
2. Executive Summary (BLUF + 3-5 key findings with priority)
3. Methodology (compact: who/what/when/how/sample/limitations)
4. Findings (ordered by priority or narrative arc; each with evidence)
5. Insights (the "so what" — interpretation)
6. Recommendations (prioritized 2-col table with owner and effort/impact)
7. Next Steps / Open Questions
8. Appendix (participant demographics, discussion guide, additional quotes)
```

#### Engineering variant (6-10 pages)
```
1. Header + RACI
2. BLUF
3. Failure modes summary (priority matrix)
4. Methodology (compact)
5. Behavior findings (task-level, with device/OS/error-state detail)
6. Edge cases observed
7. Recommendations (bug-like: repro steps, priority, owner)
8. Appendix (full error catalog, session recordings index)
```

#### Research team variant (15-25 pages)
```
1. Header + RACI
2. Executive Summary
3. Methodology (full — design rationale, what worked/didn't)
4. Findings (all, with full evidence trail)
5. Insights
6. Recommendations
7. Methodological learnings for next study
8. Open Questions
9. Appendix (discussion guide, coded data, affinity diagrams, analyst notes)
```

Present the structure in a short table. Ask: "Does this structure look right? Anything to add, remove, or reorder?"

### Step 3: Researcher Reviews & Approves

Use AskUserQuestion:

**Q — Approve structure?**
- "Looks good — generate the report" (Recommended)
- "Adjust sections" — researcher specifies what to add/remove/reorder
- "Switch audience variant" — loop back to Step 2 with a different audience

### Step 3.5: Style Reference (REQUIRED before generating)

After structure is approved, confirm output style. Use AskUserQuestion:

**Q — Output Style**
> "Last check before I generate — I want the formatting to match your preferred style."

Options:
- **"Use the default Instacart template" (Recommended)** — 2-column tables, section bars, RACI header, emoji priority indicators
- **"I'll share a reference doc"** — paste a previous report URL or text
- **"Just give me a clean outline"** — simple markdown headers + bullets, no tables

**If the researcher shares a reference doc:**

1. Read it (Glean `read_document`, `google-docs:fetch-google-doc`, or Read tool for pasted text)
2. Extract style patterns: header hierarchy, table use, bullet style, evidence formatting (inline quotes vs. callouts), tone (formal/conversational), level of detail
3. Confirm read-back:
   > "Here's the style I picked up:
   > - **Structure:** [e.g., numbered sections with section bars]
   > - **Findings format:** [e.g., bold H3 + evidence block + insight callout]
   > - **Recommendations:** [e.g., 6-col table with owner + priority + effort]
   > - **Tone:** [e.g., direct, business-framed]
   > Does this match? Anything to adjust?"
4. Generate using their style, not the default

**If default Instacart template:** Use the OUTPUT TEMPLATES below verbatim.

**If clean outline:** Use simple markdown — H2 headers, bullet findings, inline evidence, numbered recs.

### Step 4: Generate the Report

Produce the report using the approved structure and style. Apply the content rules in the next section. Default to the Instacart design system unless the researcher chose otherwise.

## CONTENT GENERATION RULES

1. **BLUF discipline** — The first 3 sentences answer: what / so what / now what. No methodology in the BLUF.
2. **Finding → Insight → Recommendation separation** — Never merge rungs. If the researcher's raw input mixes them, split them before writing.
3. **Evidence gate** — Every finding needs ≥1 verbatim quote OR a specific quantitative observation with denominator. Three-quote rule for strong patterns (Portigal).
4. **Participant coding** — Use P01, P02. Never names. Add one demographic tag ("P04, 34F, weekly shopper").
5. **Never clean up quotes** — Preserve "um," hedges, grammar. Cleaned quotes read as fabricated (Portigal).
6. **Numbers with denominator for small n** — "7 of 10," not "70%" when n < 20. NNG rule.
7. **Tense discipline** — Findings: past. Insights: present. Recommendations: imperative.
8. **Priority emojis lead** — 🔴 P0 / 🟡 P1 / 🟢 P2. Consistent across all tables and the exec summary.
9. **Owned recommendations** — Every rec has a named human owner. "Someone should…" is not a recommendation; it's an open question. Move it.
10. **Rec count ≤ insight count** — If recs outnumber insights, cut. Speculation flag.
11. **Caveat once** — Sample size and limitations are acknowledged in Methodology, not re-apologized for in every finding.
12. **Cut methodology from exec variant** — One paragraph at the bottom. Execs do not need to know about the think-aloud protocol.
13. **Imperative recommendations** — "Move the badge," not "consider moving the badge."
14. **Curate evidence** — Strongest 3 quotes per finding in body. Rest to appendix.
15. **Traceability** — Every rec cites an insight #. Every insight cites finding #s. Every finding cites evidence. NNG: the chain must be visible.

## COMMON REPORT FAILURES (Sharon + NNG catalog)

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Methodology-led intro | Page 1 describes sample, not findings | Lead with BLUF. Move method to §3 (or bottom for exec). |
| Finding mislabeled as insight | "Users want X" with no interpretation | Interpret in context or demote to finding. |
| Unowned recommendation | "We should consider…" | Assign named owner or move to Open Questions. |
| Evidence-less claim | Finding with no quote/clip | Add evidence or soften language ("some participants"). |
| Audience mismatch | Exec reading a 15-page doc | Re-scope to variant. Exec gets 1-2 pages. |
| Over-hedging | Every finding caveated individually | Caveat once in Methodology. |
| Clip overload | 12 quotes per finding | Curate to 3 hero quotes. Rest in appendix. |
| No open questions | Report reads as final word | Every study has follow-ons. List them. |
| Rec-to-insight inflation | 12 recs from 6 insights | Cut to ≤1:1 ratio. |
| Sanitized quotes | Every quote perfect grammar | Restore verbatim. Authenticity matters. |

## Step 4.5: Self-Critique Checklist (AIxUXR Playbook — Report Critic)

Before presenting the draft to the researcher or offering upload, run the report through the Report Critic framework from the AIxUXR Playbook (Avramenko & Minderler, 2025). This is a structured self-review — the AI's equivalent of a peer review pass. Do this *silently* and surface only the items that fail or need researcher attention.

Adopt three personas in sequence (Staff/Sr Sparring Partner → Product Manager → Senior Leadership) and check the report against each dimension.

### Report Critic — Checklist

| Dimension | Pass criteria |
|-----------|---------------|
| **BLUF presence** | First 3 sentences answer what / so what / now what. No methodology leads. A reader who stops after paragraph 1 can still decide. |
| **Finding → Insight → Recommendation ladder** | Each rung is distinct. No finding labeled as an insight. No recommendation without a traceable insight parent. Tense discipline intact (past / present / imperative). |
| **Evidence depth** | Every finding carries ≥1 verbatim quote OR a quantitative observation with denominator. Strong patterns have 3 quotes (Portigal's rule). No sanitized grammar. |
| **Participant attribution** | P-coding (P01, P02…) used throughout. One demographic tag per quote. No names. No PII. |
| **Actionability of recommendations** | Every rec has a named human owner, priority emoji, effort estimate, impact estimate, and cites its parent insight. "We should consider…" does not appear. |
| **Audience-fit** | The variant matches the primary audience from Q2. Exec variant ≤ 2 pages. Engineering variant leads with failure modes, not user quotes. Research-team variant carries full methodology. |
| **Business framing (for Exec & Product-PM)** | P0/P1 recs have a Business Framing row: what it unlocks, cost of delay, OKR alignment. No fabricated dollar figures. |
| **Visual hierarchy** | 2-column tables as default content layout. Emoji priorities lead. Section bars for H2s. No dense paragraphs (>3 lines → convert to bullets or a table). |
| **Executive scannability** | A skimmer reading only headers, bolded phrases, and the first cell of each table gets the argument. Bolded keywords guide the scan. |
| **Rec-to-insight ratio** | Recommendations ≤ insights. If recs outnumber insights, cut — that is speculation. |
| **Hedging discipline** | Sample limitations caveated once in Methodology, not re-apologized for in every finding. |
| **Data-centric grounding** | Every claim traces to provided data. No external facts, no invented quotes, no unsupported metric claims. If the researcher didn't give a number, the report doesn't manufacture one. |
| **Transparency (H.E.A.R.T. "T")** | AI assist is attributable. The researcher is the accountable human author. The report does not pretend to be anything other than a researcher-validated deliverable. |
| **Confidence calibration** | Where data is thin or ambiguous, the report says so. "Indicative, not statistically generalizable" beats false precision. |
| **Open questions surfaced** | Every study has follow-ons. If the Open Questions section is empty, the report is over-claiming. |

### Critic personas to apply

Borrowed from the AIxUXR Playbook's Prompts 1b, 2, and 3 (Loosbrock, 2025). Run each pass silently:

| Persona | What they check | What they'd ask |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| **Staff/Senior Sparring Partner** | Strategic integrity, method→goal alignment, decision framework | "What pre-defined decision thresholds does this report enable?" |
| **Product Manager (skeptical)** | Actionability, connection to roadmap, KPI relevance | "What in this report will change what my team ships next sprint?" |
| **Senior Leadership / Director** | Strategic alignment, resource justification, opportunity cost | "Which OKR does this ladder to? What's the cost of *not* acting?" |

If any checklist item fails or any persona question can't be answered from the report as written, flag it in the delivery message to the researcher: *"Before you ship this, note: [failing item]. Want me to strengthen it?"*

## Step 5: Offer Google Docs Upload

After generating the report, ask:

> "Your research report is ready. Would you like me to upload it to Google Docs with the Instacart design system applied?"

If yes:
1. Use `gws-docs` or `gws-docs-write` to create the doc
2. Organize into the appropriate Google Drive project folder (Project 1 / 2 / 3 per the researcher's study)
3. **Fix subscript formatting (MANDATORY)** — `<br>` handling in the upload pipeline creates SUBSCRIPT runs that shrink text. After upload, scan the doc via Docs API for `baselineOffset == 'SUBSCRIPT'` and reset to `'NONE'`. Do this BEFORE styling.
4. Apply `style-gdoc-full.py` to get Instacart styling: dark-green section bars, RACI chips, 2-col table widths, 6pt spaceBelow + 120% lineSpacing in table cells, 36pt above H2/H3, emoji preserved.
5. Share the Google Doc link with the researcher.

If no, leave the markdown as the final deliverable.

## Differentiation from `jedida-reporting`

`jedida-reporting` is Jedida's personal failure-analysis and pipeline-metrics reporting skill, scoped to her specific HITL/golden-dataset projects with canonical metrics, palette, and Slack formatting rules. It is Jedida-only.

`/report` is the team-facing, audience-aware research report generator for the whole Instacart Research team. It enforces the NNG/Sharon/Portigal methodology canon and the finding→insight→recommendation discipline. It does not carry Jedida-specific metrics, links, or Slack rules. Use `/report` for any stakeholder readout; use `jedida-reporting` only for Jedida's HITL pipeline deliverables.
