# Probing Rule Cheatsheet

Per SKILL.md Rule 8 (probing) and Rule 13 (standard verbal follow-up wording).

## When to probe

Probe ONLY when a verbal answer is short or thin. If the participant gave a full, specific answer, don't probe — probing a complete answer reads as "that wasn't good enough" and makes participants pad future answers with filler.

## How to probe — max once

One probe per question, maximum:

```
"Anything else stand out about [the specific thing they were reacting to]?"
```

If the participant says "I don't know," "nope," or otherwise stops after the probe — move on immediately. Don't probe a probe.

## Soft vs. accusatory wording — the one rule that matters most here

| Situation | ❌ Accusatory (triggers defensiveness) | ✅ Soft (gets honest signal) |
|---|---|---|
| Participant didn't mention a specific concept you expected | "Why didn't you mention [X]?" | "How would you handle [X]?" |
| Participant seems to be abandoning the flow | "Would you back out / abandon / give up here?" | "Would you no longer [shop/use/continue] here?" |
| Participant is asked to justify a prior tap/drag/spoken choice | "Why did you say that?" / "Why that one?" | "State your reason out loud. Why did you pick the previous option? Please give a verbal response." |

The pattern across all three: never imply the participant did something wrong (missing a concept, quitting, being inconsistent). Frame every follow-up as neutral curiosity about their experience, not as an audit of their behavior.

## Standard verbal follow-up — use verbatim

For every verbal "why?" that immediately follows a SINGLE CHOICE or DRAG-TO-RANK question:

```
State your reason out loud. Why did you pick the previous option? Please give a verbal response.
```

Why "previous option" and not "that one" or "your answer": it's platform-agnostic — works whether the prior question was a tap, a drag, or itself spoken. Vague forms ("Why did you say that?") make participants forget what they just said, especially after a multi-item ranking.

## Quick decision tree

```
Answer given
  │
  ├─ Full, specific, addresses the question → don't probe, move to next Q
  │
  ├─ Short / vague / one word
  │     │
  │     ├─ First time this happened this question → probe once, soft wording
  │     │
  │     └─ Already probed once → move on, don't probe twice
  │
  └─ Participant missed a concept you wanted covered
        → do NOT ask "why didn't you mention X" (accusatory)
        → ask "how would you handle X" instead (soft, still gets the signal)
```
